1. Introduction and Scope

This page sets out the principles and policies that guide how Leafspire works with communities, schools, volunteers, participants, supporters and partners. It covers this website, our communications and social media, community and school activities, volunteering, fundraising and collaborations.

It applies to visitors, volunteers, programme participants, community representatives, donors, supporters, partners and anyone acting on Leafspire’s behalf.

General website use and specific participation

You can read this website without agreeing to anything formally. Using it is subject to Section 13. Joining a programme, volunteering, representing Leafspire, donating or partnering may involve further requirements, such as registration or consent forms, parent or guardian permission, or a written agreement. Where one of those documents applies, we will explain it when it is relevant, and the more specific document governs that activity.

Nothing on this page asks anyone to give up rights they hold under Kenyan law. Consent to the use of personal data is requested separately where the law requires it (Section 14).

2. Organisational Identity and Legal Status

Leafspire is a youth-led social impact initiative rooted in Kenya. Its work sits where environmental sustainability and climate action, youth leadership and community participation, and community wellbeing and social connection meet, and helps young people and communities turn learning and shared responsibility into practical local action.

Current status. Leafspire is not yet registered as an NGO, public benefit organisation, community-based organisation, charity, foundation, company, trust or society, and it is not an incorporated body. Contributions to Leafspire are not described as tax-deductible.

Leafspire intends to formalise its structure as it grows. Any future registration will be announced on this website. Until then, nothing here claims registration, charitable status, regulatory approval or institutional protections that have not been established. This page does not create a legal entity or give Leafspire authority to carry out regulated activities. For significant collaborations or funding, we encourage written agreements so each party understands who is responsible for what.

3. Non-Partisanship and Responsible Engagement

Leafspire’s activities are not intended to promote any political party, candidate or electoral campaign, and we do not endorse or fundraise for them.

This is not a ban on talking about public issues. Environmental protection, youth participation, community needs, public health and sustainable development are matters of public interest, and discussing them, learning about them and encouraging people to take part in civic life are part of our work.

  • Non-partisan activity: Leafspire events, platforms and materials are not used to support or oppose parties or candidates.
  • Civic participation and public dialogue: respectful discussion, community consultation and responsible advocacy on public-interest issues are welcome. Advocacy is not the same as party-political campaigning.
  • Personal views: individuals may hold and express political views in their private capacity, provided they do not present them as Leafspire’s position.
  • Unauthorised use of our identity: using Leafspire’s name, logo, events or channels for party-political campaigning is not permitted.

4. Governance and Accountability Principles

These are the principles Leafspire commits to work by:

  • Integrity and responsible conduct: acting honestly and keeping our commitments.
  • Transparency and accountability: being open about what we do, what we know and what we do not yet have in place.
  • Responsible use of resources: using money, time and materials for their stated purposes.
  • Respectful community engagement: listening to communities and schools and working with them, not around them.
  • Youth participation and inclusion: welcoming young people of all backgrounds into leadership and decisions.
  • Appropriate oversight: making significant decisions collectively and with care.
  • Ethical collaboration: working with partners who share these standards.
  • Safeguarding and protection: putting the safety of children and vulnerable people first.
  • Responsible communication: representing our work, partners and results accurately.

Where things stand

Leafspire is led by its founding team, who are listed on our Our Team page. This page does not claim that a board of trustees, governance committee, external audit or similar oversight body exists. These are areas we intend to formalise as Leafspire develops, and we will update this page when that happens.

5. Code of Conduct

Everyone representing or taking part in Leafspire activities is expected to:

  • communicate respectfully, online and in person;
  • treat everyone fairly and not discriminate on grounds such as gender, ethnicity, religion, disability, age, background or income;
  • never harass, intimidate, threaten, bully or abuse anyone, including through messages and social media;
  • respect personal boundaries, and stop when someone asks;
  • use Leafspire’s name, logo and channels only as authorised;
  • keep confidential information confidential, and handle other people’s details with care;
  • behave appropriately at community and school activities, follow the host’s rules, and never be alone with a child in a way that breaches Section 7;
  • respect property, public spaces, natural resources and community assets; and
  • raise concerns promptly and cooperate honestly and fairly with any review.

Behaviour that breaks this code may lead to a warning, removal from an activity, withdrawal of permission to represent Leafspire, and, where the law requires or the situation is serious, referral to the relevant authorities.

6. Volunteers, Participants and Representatives

This section applies to volunteers, programme participants, community representatives and anyone acting for Leafspire.

  • Taking part does not by itself create employment. If an arrangement is in fact an employment relationship, the legal obligations that come with it apply regardless of how it is described.
  • Nobody may speak for Leafspire, sign agreements, collect money or promise funding, jobs, scholarships or other benefits without clear authorisation from the founding team.
  • Some activities have their own requirements, such as age limits, training, consent forms or safeguarding checks. These will be explained honestly before you commit.
  • Confidentiality, safeguarding and conduct expectations apply throughout.
  • Leafspire does not guarantee jobs, admission, funding or other outcomes from participation.

Arrangements, including expectations about time, costs and what Leafspire can and cannot provide, should be communicated clearly and truthfully.

7. Child Safeguarding and Protection

Leafspire’s work may involve children, schools and young people. We have zero tolerance for abuse, exploitation, harassment and neglect, and everyone representing Leafspire must put children’s safety and best interests first, consistent with the Constitution of Kenya, 2010 and the Children Act, 2022.

  • Boundaries and dignity: maintain professional boundaries, respect each child’s dignity and privacy, and never use a position of trust to exploit a child.
  • Safe communication: communicate with children openly and appropriately, in group or visible settings or with a parent, guardian or teacher aware, and not through private channels.
  • Permissions: obtain parent or guardian permission, and school approval where relevant, before activities that call for it.
  • Images: do not photograph, record or publish identifiable images of a child without appropriate consent, and do not share images of children on personal accounts.
  • Reporting: report any concern at once. Reports are handled confidentially, shared only with those who need to know, and referred to the appropriate authorities where required.
  • No retaliation: no one will be penalised for raising a concern in good faith.

If a child is in immediate danger, contact the police or emergency services first. In Kenya, the national child helpline is 116.

These are Leafspire’s safeguarding commitments. This short section does not replace a full Child Safeguarding and Protection Policy, and it does not claim that a designated safeguarding officer, reporting hotline, vetting system or referral partnership is in place. We will publish the full policy and its procedures once adopted.

To report a concern to Leafspire, email leafspirefoundation@gmail.com with “Safeguarding concern” in the subject line, or call +254 701 114562.

8. Anti-Fraud, Impersonation and Misrepresentation

Fraudulent use of Leafspire’s identity harms the people it targets and the trust our work depends on. The following are not permitted:

  • raising money or collecting goods in Leafspire’s name without written authorisation;
  • pretending to be a Leafspire founder, volunteer or representative;
  • falsely claiming to be affiliated with or endorsed by Leafspire;
  • misusing Leafspire’s logo, name or materials; and
  • promising funding, jobs, scholarships, placements or programme benefits that Leafspire has not offered.

Be cautious of any request for money, personal details or fees that claims to come from Leafspire, especially if it creates urgency or asks you to pay into a personal account. Verify it first through the contact details on this website: leafspirefoundation@gmail.com or +254 701 114562, or our Contact page. If you think you have seen a scam or impersonation, report it to us and, where money was lost, to your payment provider and the police. We cannot guarantee that every impersonation attempt can be prevented.

9. Donations and Donor Policy

  • Voluntary: all contributions are voluntary gifts to support Leafspire’s work. Nothing is owed in return, and a contribution does not guarantee any particular outcome.
  • Intended use: contributions support Leafspire’s programmes, such as youth leadership, environmental and community projects, and the practical costs of delivering them. Where a contribution is given for a stated purpose, we will try to honour it. If that is not possible, we will contact the donor to agree an alternative, and will not simply redirect it.
  • Accurate descriptions: fundraising descriptions should state truthfully what is being raised and for what, without exaggeration.
  • Responsible use: funds should be used for their stated purposes, carefully and with proper records.
  • Acknowledgement: we will acknowledge contributions and share updates on how they have helped. Anonymous donors can say so.
  • Payment channels: only use payment channels published by Leafspire on this website, and do not send money to personal accounts or numbers shared by others (Section 8).
  • Fundraising by others: no one may fundraise in Leafspire’s name without written authorisation. Fundraising can also be regulated by Kenyan law, so we ask anyone who wants to organise a fundraiser to contact us first.
  • Reporting: Leafspire is still developing its reporting. Reports to donors are updates on activities and use of funds. They are not audited financial statements unless we say so. Donors may ask us questions about how their contribution was used.

Contributions are not described as tax-deductible. Donors who need tax advice should seek it independently.

10. Refunds and Payment Errors

A contribution is a gift, but mistakes happen and we will treat refund enquiries fairly. You may ask for a review if:

  • the same payment was made more than once;
  • the amount paid was not the amount you intended;
  • the transaction was unauthorised or fraudulent; or
  • a technical fault caused an incorrect payment.

You may also ask to cancel or reverse a contribution. We will consider the request, but if the money has already been committed or spent on an activity, it may not be possible to return it. Payment providers such as banks and mobile-money services have their own rules, and may handle disputed or erroneous transactions directly. We encourage you to contact your provider as well.

To make an enquiry, email leafspirefoundation@gmail.com or use our Contact page. Include your name, the date, amount, payment method, the transaction reference or receipt, and what went wrong. We will review each enquiry individually and reply as soon as we reasonably can. We cannot promise that a refund will be approved or set a fixed timeframe. This section does not limit any rights you have under Kenyan law or the rules of your payment provider.

11. Partnerships and Collaborations

We work with schools, community groups, other organisations and individuals. We expect all collaborations to follow these principles:

  • Describe a partnership accurately. Using Leafspire’s identity does not by itself create an official partnership or endorsement, and a partnership exists only if Leafspire has confirmed it.
  • Use names, logos and branding only with permission and as agreed.
  • Agree roles and responsibilities clearly, in writing where appropriate, especially for funding, shared activities, data or work with children.
  • Act ethically and respect safeguarding requirements, including Section 7.
  • Handle shared information only for the agreed purpose and protect it appropriately.
  • Declare any conflict of interest early and manage it openly.

Leafspire may end a collaboration if these principles are not respected.

12. Intellectual Property and Content Use

Leafspire’s name, logo and original written and visual materials are protected, and should not be copied, altered or presented as yours without permission. Some content on this website, such as photographs, may belong to others or be used under licence, and we do not claim ownership of third-party material.

  • You may share links to our pages and quote short extracts with credit. Anything beyond that, including using our branding, needs written permission.
  • Do not claim Leafspire’s work as your own or suggest we have endorsed yours.
  • If you send us text, images or other material, you confirm that you have the right to share it. We will use it only for the purpose you agreed.
  • Photographs and recordings of people are published only with appropriate permission (see Sections 7 and 14).

To ask permission, or to tell us that something on this website has been used without proper authority, write to leafspirefoundation@gmail.com.

13. Website Use, Disclaimers and Liability

We try to keep the information on this website accurate and current, but it may contain errors or be out of date, and the site may sometimes be unavailable. Please check with us before relying on it for something important, such as event details.

  • External sites and services: we link to and use third-party websites and services. We do not control them and are not responsible for their content or practices.
  • Voluntary activities: taking part is voluntary. Please take reasonable care of yourself and others, follow safety instructions, and make your own informed decisions. Parents and guardians are responsible for deciding whether activities are suitable for their children.
  • Impersonation and fraud: we cannot control third parties who falsely use our name. Please follow the verification steps in Section 8.
  • Events beyond our control: we are not responsible for delays, cancellations or failures caused by circumstances we could not reasonably control, such as severe weather, public emergencies or network failures.

Leafspire accepts responsibility where it is at fault. Nothing on this page excludes or limits liability or rights that cannot lawfully be excluded or limited under Kenyan law, and any limitation here applies only to the extent the law allows.

14. Privacy and Personal Data Protection

Leafspire respects the right to privacy under Article 31 of the Constitution of Kenya, 2010 and aims to handle personal data in line with the Data Protection Act, 2019 and its regulations. This section is a summary, not a complete privacy notice. A fuller notice is a recommended next step.

What we may collect and why

  • Newsletter: if you subscribe, we collect your email address to send updates about our programmes, events and stories. The subscription form is processed by Formspree, a third-party service.
  • Enquiries, volunteering and participation: details you choose to give us, such as your name, contact details, school or community, and messages, used to respond, organise activities and keep participants safe.
  • Photographs and video: taken at activities for reporting and communication, with appropriate consent, and with parental or guardian consent for children.
  • Technical data: this website loads fonts, icons and scripts from third-party providers (including Google Fonts, cdnjs, jsDelivr and jQuery). When they serve those files, they may receive your IP address and browser details. Some pages link to platforms such as YouTube and social media, which have their own policies.

How we handle it

  • Minimisation and purpose: we aim to collect only what we need and use it only for the purpose we told you.
  • Consent and other lawful bases: we rely on your consent where required, which you can withdraw, or on another lawful basis the Act allows.
  • Security and retention: we take reasonable care to protect data and keep it only as long as needed, then delete or anonymise it.
  • Sharing: we do not sell personal data. We may share it with service providers who help us run our website or activities, with partners where you have been told, and with authorities where the law requires.
  • Children’s data: the law places additional limits on processing children’s personal data, and generally requires a parent’s or guardian’s consent. We collect only what is necessary and take particular care with names, images and school details.

Your rights

You have rights under the Data Protection Act, 2019, including to be informed about how your data is used, to access it, to object to its processing, and to ask for it to be corrected or deleted. To use these rights, or raise a privacy concern, email leafspirefoundation@gmail.com with “Privacy” in the subject line. If you are not satisfied with our response, you may complain to the Office of the Data Protection Commissioner.
